📘 Free Amazon Buying Guide

Learn the tricks sellers use to make bad deals look good — and how to avoid them.

1. The Fake MRP Trick

Sellers inflate MRP to show "70% OFF". Real MRP = what the brand officially lists. Check the brand's website or compare across 3+ sellers.

✅ Our fix: We flag suspicious MRPs on every product page.

2. The "Amazon's Choice" Trap

"Amazon's Choice" often just means "sells fast" — not "best quality". It's algorithmic, not editorial. Ignore the badge; read the 1-2★ reviews instead.

3. Review Manipulation Patterns

  • Sudden spike of 5★ reviews with identical phrasing
  • Reviews mentioning "fast delivery" but not the product
  • No photos/videos in 5★ reviews, but 1★ reviews have photos of defects
  • "Vine Voice" or "Early Reviewer" badges on suspicious clusters

4. Price History Matters

A "deal" today might be the regular price last month. Use tools like Keepa or CamelCamelCamel (or our daily price checks) to see 90-day history.

5. The "Limited Time" Pressure

Countdown timers and "Deal ends in 2 hours" are often reset daily. Real lightning deals have a fixed end timestamp. If it's been "ending soon" for 3 days, it's fake.

6. Check the Seller

  • "Sold by Amazon" or "Fulfilled by Amazon" = safest
  • Third-party sellers: check their rating (>95%) and return policy
  • Avoid "Just Launched" sellers with 0 feedback

7. Category-Specific Red Flags

  • Air fryers: "Digital display" but no presets listed = basic timer only
  • Mixer grinders: "750W" but copper motor not specified = cheaper aluminum
  • Earbuds: "ANC" without dB rating = usually passive noise isolation only
  • Smartwatches: "Blood oxygen" without medical disclaimer = toy sensor
  • Power banks: "20000mAh" at ₹500 = fake capacity (real: ₹1500+)

Bookmark This Checklist

Before any Amazon purchase:

  1. Check 90-day price history
  2. Read 5 recent 1-2★ reviews (photos = bonus)
  3. Verify MRP on brand website
  4. Confirm "Sold by Amazon" or top-rated seller
  5. Check return window (10 days minimum)
